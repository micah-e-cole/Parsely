Hush now the teacups tumble and the moon hums softly in a kettle, while buttons hopscotch over banisters and spoons remember lullabies they never learned. The sheep count themselves backwards, woolly commas drifting across a sky stitched with giggles. A cradle rocks a cloud, a cloud rocks a cradle, and somewhere a paper crown yawns. Little boots march in circles around a pie that forgot it was baked, singing la la lavender, la la later, la la maybe tomorrow. Stars blink like marbles lost under couches, waiting to be found by fingers sticky with jam and time.

Ring a rosy apple core, pocketfuls of maybe, ashes whisper stories to the floorboards. The clock hiccups, the clock hums, the clock decides to nap. Jack be nimble, Jack be noodle, Jack be a ladder made of licorice and hope. Over the candlestick of dusk he trips and lands in a puddle of rhyme. Rain pats the roof like a grandmother counting breaths, slow and warm, and the wind ties bows in the curtains. Bees hum bedtime, buzz hush, buzz hush, buzz hush, while honey drips punctuation on the page.

Little miss tuffet sits on a footstool made of marshmallow memory, curds and whey arguing about who came first. A spider knits a mitten, then unknits it, then knits the idea of knitting. The moon wears a bonnet crooked with pride. Down comes the cradle, down comes the rhyme, down comes the alphabet sliding like sleds down a hill of snow-white consonants. Mother Goose winks and shuffles her deck of geese, feathers fluttering like confetti.

Humpty Dumpty hums a tune shaped like an egg, round and thoughtful, leaning against a wall that listens. When he falls, it is more of a sigh than a crash, a punctuation mark at the end of a sentence that keeps going anyway. All the king’s horses neigh in iambs, all the king’s men clap off-beat, and together they sweep the mess into a basket labeled tomorrow. The sun peeks, then hides, then peeks again, practicing peekaboo with the horizon.

Twinkle the twine of twilight, little starfish on the ceiling, how I wonder what you aren’t. Up above the world so weary, like a diamond dropped in cereal, floating milkwards, soggy with wonder. The cow jumps over the moon but forgets to land, drifting in a slow-motion moo that echoes through pajamas. The dish runs away with the spoon, but they come back for tea, apologizing with clinks.

Pat-a-cake the baker’s rhyme, bake me a story soft as bread. Mark it with a B for bedtime, put it in the oven of now. When it’s done, tap twice, listen for hollow laughter. Sing a song of sixpence, pockets full of crumbs, four-and-twenty blackbirds practicing jazz inside a pie that taps its foot. When the pie is opened, the birds fly out in orderly chaos, feathered semicolons.

Hey diddle diddle, the cat and the fiddle share a secret chord. The fiddle fiddles back, the cat purrs punctuation, and the moon laughs a silver laugh that tickles rooftops. The little dog giggles to see such sport, tail wagging in rhyme-time, while plates applaud and forks bow. Every sound becomes a pillow.

Baa baa black sheep, have you any wool, asks the meadow politely. Yes sir yes sir three bags full of stories, answers the sheep, distributing chapters to the lane, the dame, and the child who lives in a shoe-shaped paragraph. That child ties laces around verbs and runs downhill into a laugh.

Rock-a-bye baby on the treetop of thought, when the wind blows the cradle will sway like a hammock of vowels. When the bough breaks, it doesn’t break, it bends into a question mark and asks if falling can be gentle. The answer hums itself to sleep.

Old Mother Hubbard opens a cupboard of clouds, finds nothing but echoes, feeds the dog a rhyme instead. The dog wags and eats the echo, leaving crumbs of meaning on the rug. Little Bo Peep loses her sheep, then finds them hiding in metaphors, tails wagging behind bushes of rhyme. Leave them alone and they’ll come home, trailing jingles like bells on boots.

Peter Piper picks a peck of pickled pictures, peppers the page with pop. How many pictures did Peter pick? Enough to wallpaper a dream. Simple Simon meets a pieman walking to the fair of fairies, asks for a taste of maybe, is offered a slice of later. The pieman smiles with flour-dusted teeth.
